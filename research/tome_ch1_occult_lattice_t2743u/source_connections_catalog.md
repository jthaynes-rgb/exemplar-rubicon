# Source connections catalog

Every outside source cited in this pack is listed here. Densify can weave from this list and keep a matching list of its own. If a note cites a source that is not in this file, the pack is incomplete. If this file names a URL that none of the notes cite, the catalog has drifted.

In-repo blueprint files (`ARCHITECTURE.md`, `docs/DRIFT.md`, `docs/SEAL.md`, `SECURITY.md`, the repository `README.md`) are public loci, not outside sources. They are mapped in `crossref_hints.md` and are not given S-numbers.

Works named inside a cited page but not opened as their own text are marked **not opened** under the entry that names them. They are not separate citations.

Read in this pass: October 2026. “Page read” means the URL was opened and the claim in the notes comes from that page or from a rule that page states and a spot-check confirmed. “Contents only” means a contents list or index was read, not every chapter.

## A. Letter classes and Hebrew number

### S01 — Westcott, *Sepher Yetzirah*

- **Cite:** Westcott, William Wynn, trans. *Sepher Yetzirah* (*The Book of Formation*). English text on Wikisource, “Sepher Yetzirah/Sepher Yetzirah.”
- **URL:** https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Sepher_Yetzirah/Sepher_Yetzirah
- **Use:** Three mothers (Aleph, Mem, Shin = air, water, fire; air as tongue of the balance; chest, belly, head), seven double letters and their pairs, twelve simple letters and their properties. The planet *list* is in the text; a one-letter-one-planet lock is not, in the passage read.
- **Cited in:** `tarot_lattice.md`
- **Not opened:** Aryeh Kaplan’s edition. Do not cite it from this pack.

### S02 — Hebrew gematria methods (Wikipedia)

- **Cite:** “Gematria.” *Wikipedia*. Methods section: mispar hechrachi, mispar gadol, mispar katan, mispar siduri, mispar katan mispari, and related variants.
- **URL:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gematria
- **Use:** Standard 1–400 values and the names of the classical operations that English EO/FR/RO only resemble.
- **Cited in:** `gematria_methods.md`

### S03 — TorahCalc, gematria methods

- **Cite:** TorahCalc. “Explanations of Gematria Methods with Charts.” Includes mispar hechrachi, mispar siduri, mispar katan, and atbash (Aleph with Tav, Beth with Shin).
- **URL:** https://www.torahcalc.com/info/gematria
- **Use:** Plain definitions, especially atbash as exchange with the opposite letter.
- **Cited in:** `gematria_methods.md`

### S04 — Aish, “What Is Gematria?”

- **Cite:** Aish. “What Is Gematria?” Overview of mispar hechrachi, mispar gadol, mispar siduri, mispar katan, mispar perati, mispar shemi, and atbash.
- **URL:** https://aish.com/what-is-gematria/
- **Use:** A second plain-language witness for the same Hebrew method names.
- **Cited in:** `gematria_methods.md`

### S05 — GalEinai, four methods

- **Cite:** GalEinai / Inner.org. “Four Methods for Doing Gematria.” Absolute value (mispar hechrachi), including the note on final letters.
- **URL:** https://inner.org/gematria/fourways.php
- **Use:** Absolute value stated from a traditional teaching page, final-letter caution included.
- **Cited in:** `gematria_methods.md`

## B. Golden Dawn tarot and *Liber 777*

### S06 — Mathers and Felkin, *Book T* (circulating PDF)

- **Cite:** Mathers, S. L. MacGregor, and Harriet Felkin. *Book T — The Tarot, Comprising Manuscripts N, O, P, Q, R, and an Unlettered Theoricus Adeptus Minor Instruction.* PDF hosted by Benebell Wen, filename dated 1888. Secondary notes date the paper to about 1891 and attribute it in the main to Mathers (see S07, S08).
- **URL:** https://benebellwen.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/02/mathers-and-felkin-golden-dawn-book-t-the-tarot-1888.pdf
- **Use:** Order paper for trump attributions and minor titles. Spot-checked: Fool as spiritual idea versus material folly; early divinatory tones; fragments “Aries Established Strength 3 of Wands,” “Scorpio Pleasure 6 of Cups,” “Capricorn Earthly Power 4 of Pentacles,” “Virgo Prudence 8 of Pentacles,” “Gemini Ruin 10 of Swords”; mothers, doubles, and simples pictured on the Ace-of-Wands torch description. Not a full transcription of the 78-card paper.
- **Cited in:** `tarot_lattice.md`

### S07 — Steve-P, Golden Dawn tarot note

- **Cite:** Steve-P. “Cards — The Golden Dawn Tarot.” States that *Book T* was anonymously written around 1891, in the main by Mathers, and that Regardie’s Llewellyn compilation is the usual paper edition.
- **URL:** https://steve-p.org/cards/GDaw.html
- **Use:** Dating and publication path for *Book T*. Not a second attribution table.
- **Cited in:** `tarot_lattice.md`
- **Not opened:** Israel Regardie, *The Golden Dawn* (Llewellyn) and *The Complete Golden Dawn System of Magic*. Named here only as the books S07 points to.

## D. Agrippa, Lilly, planetary hours

### S24 — Agrippa, Book II (Peterson transcription)

- **Cite:** Agrippa, Heinrich Cornelius. *Three Books of Occult Philosophy*. English translation, London, 1651. Book II, Joseph H. Peterson transcription, esotericarchives.com. The planetary-hours procedure is the sunrise-to-sunset and sunset-to-sunrise division into twelve equal parts, first hour to the lord of the day, then the order of succession; plus the alternative of fifteen degrees of oblique ascension.
- **URL:** https://www.esotericarchives.com/agrippa/op2.htm
- **Use:** Primary procedure for unequal planetary hours, and a warning that Book II also contains seals, intelligences, and images which this pack does **not** copy.
- **Cited in:** `planetary_thelema.md`

### S25 — Agrippa, Wikisource contents

- **Cite:** Agrippa, Heinrich Cornelius. *Three Books of Occult Philosophy*. Wikisource contents. Book II, chapter 34 titled “Of the true motion of the heavenly bodies to be observed in the eighth sphere, & of the ground of Planetary hours.” Neighboring chapters (26–33 in that contents list) cover celestial agreement, proportion of the body, harmony of the soul, when planets are most powerful, fixed stars, Sun and Moon, and the twenty-eight lunar mansions.
- **URL:** https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Three_Books_of_Occult_Philosophy
- **Use:** Chapter title and the warrant that Book II is measure and timing. Those neighboring chapters were not transcribed.
- **Cited in:** `planetary_thelema.md`, `gematria_methods.md`

### S26 — Agrippa, 1651 EEBO, Book II chapter 34

- **URL:** https://quod.lib.umich.edu/e/eebo/A26565.0001.001/1:16.34?rgn=div2&view=fulltext

### S27 — Wikipedia, *Three Books of Occult Philosophy*

### S28 — Renaissance Astrology, planetary hours

- **Cite:** Warnock, Christopher. “Planetary Hours and Days.” RenaissanceAstrology.com. Chaldean order as relative orbital velocity; seasonal hours; day lords; citation to William Lilly, *Christian Astrology*, p. 482; Sunday walk (hour 1 Sun, hour 2 Venus, daylight ending on Saturn, night opening on Jupiter, hour 24 Mercury, Monday opening on the Moon).

### S29 — Lilly via Time-Price Research

- **Cite:** Time-Price Research. “Planetary Hours.” 15 July 2015. Quotes Lilly, *Christian Astrology*, on unequal hours: some ancients used hours of sixty minutes, but astrologers vary length with the Sun in summer and winter.

### S30 — Stockinger, planetary hours example

- **Cite:** Stockinger, Peter. “Planetary Hours.” *Peter Stockinger’s Traditional Astrology Weblog*, 17 September 2010. One Monday worked in UT: sunrise 05:58, sunset 20:15, day-hour about 70 minutes; Moon hours 05:58–07:08 and 14:12–15:22. Names the Chaldean order as the order Lilly describes.

## E. English ciphers and Bacon (kept distinct)

### S31 — Calculator-gematria, seven ciphers

- **Cite:** Gematria Calculator. “7 Cipher Systems (English & Hebrew).” Publishes English Ordinal (A=1…Z=26), Full Reduction (digits 1–9), Reverse Ordinal (A=26…Z=1), Reverse Full Reduction, and other ciphers this pack does not use (English Sumerian, a “Jewish” Latin table, Hebrew mispar hechrachi).
- **URL:** https://calculator-gematria.com/
- **Use:** Witness that EO, FR, and RO are three named ciphers among others, and that Hebrew absolute value is a separate row.
- **Cited in:** `gematria_methods.md`

### S32 — English Ordinal vs Full Reduction

- **Cite:** GematriaCalculator.us. “English Ordinal vs Full Reduction: Which Cipher Should You Use?” Published date on the page: 28 July 2026. Ordinal = A1–Z26. Full Reduction = A–I 1–9, J–R 1–9, S–Z 1–8. Warns that FR matches are common and not, by themselves, remarkable. Notes Single Reduction (S–Z left at ordinal values), Reverse Ordinal, and Reduction-KV as different variants.
- **URL:** https://www.gematriacalculator.us/blog/english-ordinal-vs-full-reduction
- **Use:** The FR folding rule and the collision warning that becomes a writing rule.
- **Cited in:** `gematria_methods.md`

### S33 — Gemater, Reverse Ordinal

- **Cite:** Gemater. “Reverse Ordinal — calculator and explainer.” A=26 … Z=1. States that Reverse = 27 − ordinal position, so Reverse sum + Simple sum = 27 × letter count, and that English atbash-as-ordinal is the same arithmetic.
- **URL:** https://www.gemater.com/methods/reverse
- **Use:** The complement identity used in the audit.
- **Cited in:** `gematria_methods.md`

### S34 — CalcForLife, letters only

- **Cite:** CalcForLife. “Gematria Calculator” (en-za). Three mappings: English ordinal A=1 to Z=26, reverse ordinal A=26 to Z=1, reduced values 1 to 9 (A–I 1–9, then J returns to 1). Non-letters ignored rather than given an invented value.
- **URL:** https://calcforlife.com/en-za/gematria-calculator/
- **Use:** The letters-only rule.
- **Cited in:** `gematria_methods.md`

### S35 — Bacon simple, reverse, and Kay ciphers

- **Cite:** Francis Bacon Research Trust. “Baconian-Rosicrucian Ciphers” (PDF). Discusses the biliteral cipher in *De Augmentis Scientiarum* (1623; English 1640) and the cabalistic Simple, Reverse, and Kay ciphers on a 24-letter alphabet (no separate J or U). Prints Simple A=1…Z=24 and Reverse A=24…Z=1. Kay is a different table (K=10).
- **URL:** https://www.fbrt.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/Baconian-Rosicrucian_Ciphers.pdf
- **Use:** Proof that Bacon’s ordinal is not English Ordinal. *Advancement of Learning* (1605) and *De Augmentis* (1623) were not opened beyond what this PDF says about them.
- **Cited in:** `gematria_methods.md`

### S36 — Bacon’s biliteral cipher

- **Cite:** “Bacon’s cipher.” *Wikipedia*. The five-character a/b biliteral code, 24-letter form with I/J and U/V doubled, and a 26-letter variant. Not a letter-sum.
- **URL:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacon%27s_cipher
- **Use:** Keeps the biliteral cipher from being confused with EO/RO.
- **Cited in:** `gematria_methods.md`
