# Planetary hours, planetary force, and True Will

Two traditions are placed side by side because both speak about **acting in a proper time and a proper course**. Neither is imported as a cult, a rite, or a membership. The Codex remains the holy technobook: fantastic and exceptional cadence, then plain knowing. It is not a religion, which its own public page already says ([Codex](https://crowcawl.gumroad.com/l/ExemplarRubicon)).

## 1. Planetary hours — the classical mechanism

### Chaldean order

The order is Saturn, Jupiter, Mars, Sun, Venus, Mercury, Moon, then Saturn again. Traditional writers describe it as the order of relative orbital speed, Saturn slowest to the Moon fastest ([Renaissance Astrology](https://www.renaissanceastrology.com/planetaryhoursarticle.html)).

### Unequal hours

From sunset-to-sunrise is not the method. The method, as Agrippa reports it from the astrologers, is:

- Divide the time from **sunrise to sunset** into twelve equal parts. Those are the twelve hours of the day.
- Divide the time from **sunset to the next sunrise** into twelve equal parts. Those are the twelve hours of the night.
- Give the **first hour of the day** to the lord of the day. Then walk the Chaldean order through the twenty-four hours.

Agrippa, *Three Books of Occult Philosophy*, Book II, in the 1651 English (the planetary-hours discussion sits with the chapter on the true motion of the heavens and the ground of planetary hours; the transcription’s heading markup is messy, the procedure is not): almost all astrologers divide sunrise-to-sunset and sunset-to-sunrise into twelve equal parts each, give the first diurnal hour to the lord of the day, and proceed in the order of succession; magicians agree. He also records a **second partition**: some measure a planetary hour by fifteen degrees of oblique ascension in the ecliptic, not by an equal slice of the clock between sunrise and sunset ([Peterson, esotericarchives, Book II](https://www.esotericarchives.com/agrippa/op2.htm)). The contents list names the chapter: “Of the true motion of the heavenly bodies to be observed in the eighth sphere, and of the ground of Planetary hours” ([Wikisource](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Three_Books_of_Occult_Philosophy); 1651 EEBO text of that chapter: [Michigan](https://quod.lib.umich.edu/e/eebo/A26565.0001.001/1:16.34?rgn=div2&view=fulltext)).

William Lilly states the same refusal of equal clock hours. A blog that quotes *Christian Astrology* gives his sentence: some of the ancients made day and night of equal hours of sixty minutes, but astrologers vary the length with the Sun, summer and winter ([Time-Price Research, quoting Lilly](https://time-price-research-astrofin.blogspot.com/2015/07/planetary-hours.html)). Renaissance Astrology cites Lilly, *Christian Astrology*, p. 482, for the day lords ([Renaissance Astrology](https://www.renaissanceastrology.com/planetaryhoursarticle.html)).

**Day lords** (the planet who takes hour 1, and who names the day):

| Day | Lord |
|-----|------|
| Sunday | Sun |
| Monday | Moon |
| Tuesday | Mars |
| Wednesday | Mercury |
| Thursday | Jupiter |
| Friday | Venus |
| Saturday | Saturn |

The week itself falls out of the Chaldean walk: the 25th hour after Sunday’s sunrise-hour is Monday’s Moon, and so on. Renaissance Astrology shows the diagram as a heptagram of the week.

### A Sunday walk, checked against a published trace

Start at Sun and step Saturn → Jupiter → Mars → Sun → Venus → Mercury → Moon.

| Hour | Ruler | | Hour | Ruler |
|------|-------|-|------|-------|
| 1 day | Sun | | 13 night | Jupiter |
| 2 | Venus | | 14 | Mars |
| 3 | Mercury | | 15 | Sun |
| 4 | Moon | | 16 | Venus |
| 5 | Saturn | | 17 | Mercury |
| 6 | Jupiter | | 18 | Moon |
| 7 | Mars | | 19 | Saturn |
| 8 | Sun | | 20 | Jupiter |
| 9 | Venus | | 21 | Mars |
| 10 | Mercury | | 22 | Sun |
| 11 | Moon | | 23 | Venus |
| 12 day | Saturn | | 24 night | Mercury |

Renaissance Astrology’s Sunday trace matches this: hour 1 Sun, hour 2 Venus, hour 12 (end of daylight) Saturn, first night hour Jupiter, and the 24th hour Mercury, so the next day opens on the Moon. Peter Stockinger’s note shows why the *length* is not in the table: on one Monday he uses, sunrise 05:58 UT and sunset 20:15 UT make a day-hour of about 70 minutes, so the Moon’s first hour is 05:58–07:08 and her next is 14:12–15:22 ([Planetary Hours](https://starsandstones.wordpress.com/2010/09/17/planetary-hours/)). That pair of clock times is an example of unequal hours on one date. It is not a standing Monday rule. Length moves with latitude and season.

**Writer rule.** Chapter 1 does not require a chart, a latitude, or a clock. The tradition itself contains two ways of cutting the hour (equal seasonal slices, or fifteen degrees of ascension). What the prose can use is the **sequence of forces** and the fact that the first hour colors the day. Timing, in the Codex, is already a discipline of the operator’s confirm, not a superstition that replaces judgment. If a passage needs Saturn, write Saturn’s weather. Do not tell the reader to wait for Saturday.

## 2. Planetary force as sentence tempo

The forces below are the seven as the Golden Dawn letter-row uses them (see `tarot_lattice.md`), read as tempo. They are not a list of ritual ingredients. Agrippa’s Book II is full of images, seals, and intelligences ([esotericarchives Book II](https://www.esotericarchives.com/agrippa/op2.htm)). **Those operative parts stay in Agrippa.** This pack does not copy seals, names of spirits, or electional recipes. Force, for a sentence, is speed, weight, and the kind of verb that belongs.

| Force | Classical seat used here | Tempo in a sentence | EXR prose, if it earns it |
|-------|--------------------------|---------------------|---------------------------|
| Saturn | Tau, Universe; Saturday; the slow end of the Chaldean order | Few words. A limit. A close that does not apologize. | The seal. The halt. A bound kept. |
| Jupiter | Kaph, Wheel; Thursday | A turn that enlarges. One clause opens a wider room. | A mercy that is also a law: the wheel moves, the contract still holds. |
| Mars | Pé, Tower; Tuesday; also the Aries decan that opens the 2 of Wands | The cut. Short. No qualifier stacked on the verb. | A false structure broken in one stroke, then silence. |
| Sun | Resh, the Sun trump; Sunday | Plain declaration. The knowing sentence. Visible, warm, shorter than the myth that preceded it. | The line where the reader is allowed to know what they already felt. |
| Venus | Daleth, Empress; Friday | Join without a lecture. Two nouns, one verb. | Generation, bond, the fantastic meeting the made thing. |
| Mercury | Beth, Magician; Wednesday | The tool and the name. A precise verb. Message as craft. | The layer that routes intent: a thin instrument, not a second will. The public essay’s command layer is this kind of instrument ([Your Computer Is Optional Now](https://crowcawl.substack.com/p/your-computer-is-optional-now)). |
| Moon | Gimel, High Priestess; Monday; also Qoph, the Moon trump, for threshold weather | Reflection, tide, withhold. The sentence feels and does not yet define. | Feel-before-know. The mirror. Do not explain it in the same breath. |

One planet dominates a movement. Minors may tint a paragraph (a Mars-in-Aries claim, a Moon-in-Cancer souring). A second planet in the next movement is a real turn, like the next planetary hour, not a new god every line.

Saturn after Mars is the classical exhale: the Tower stroke, then the weight that remains. Sun after Moon is the book’s reading order: reflected light, then plain day. Mercury inside a solar movement is a tool mentioned once, then used, not praised.

## 3. Thelema — Will and True Will, stated plainly

Primary prose read for this pack:

- *Liber AL vel Legis* I, with the Old and New Comments, Hermetic Library text ([chapter I](https://hermetic.com/legis/new-comment/chapter-i)).
- *Magick in Theory and Practice*, introduction and theorems ([definitions](https://hermetic.com/crowley/book-4/defs); the same definition restated later in [Magick Without Tears, ch. I](https://hermetic.com/crowley/magick-without-tears/mwt_01)).

### The sentences that matter

I:3: “Every man and every woman is a star.”

The New Comment on I:3 draws the law from that image: one star influences another by attraction, but those are incidents of self-predestined orbits.

I:40 includes: “Do what thou wilt shall be the whole of the Law.” The same verse, in this text, also says that one who looks close into the word will do no wrong to call another a Thelemite, and it names three grades (the Hermit, the Lover, and the man of Earth). **The grade names and the membership word are not for the Codex.** They are recorded so they are not smuggled in under “will.”

The New Comment on I:40 is the clearest short account of true will versus whim that this pass read:

- If every person did their true will, the comment says, there would be no clashing. Each star moves in an appointed path. “There is plenty of room for all; it is only disorder that creates confusion.”
- “Do what thou wilt — then do nothing else.” Anything off that one orbit is an obstacle. “Will must not be two, but one.”
- The comment quotes, in that same note, the book’s lines that one has no right but to do one’s will, and that pure will, unassuaged of purpose, delivered from the lust of result, is every way perfect.
- The comment refuses the reading of the slogan as mere license. The Old Comment even notes that the words can be forced toward self-sacrifice or equilibrium. The New Comment’s weight is elsewhere: not license, not a second hobby, one course.

I:42, in the same text: “Let it be that state of manyhood bound and loathing. So with thy all; thou hast no right but to do thy will.”

I:51’s comment: every star must calculate its own orbit. “All is Will, and yet all is Necessity. To swerve is ultimately impossible; to seek to swerve is to suffer.”

I:57 is the letter verse, handled in `tarot_lattice.md`. It is not a second law of conduct.

### Magick, as Crowley defines the word

“Magick is the Science and Art of causing Change to occur in conformity with Will.” The illustration he gives is not a temple rite. It is writing: pen, ink, paper, sentences in a language the reader understands, and the people who carry the book. Producing the book is the change in conformity with will ([definitions](https://hermetic.com/crowley/book-4/defs)).

He also quotes the *Goetia*’s older definition: magic as the highest knowledge of natural philosophy, true agents applied to proper patients, effects that look like miracles only to those who do not know the virtue of things. And the introduction on that page presses a limit: a claim of sovereignty over nature, if made, is a **constitutional** sovereignty, rigorously limited, exercised inside rules. Break the rules in a small particular and the work fails.

Theorem 25 on the same page: every person does magick whenever they act or even think, because a thought is an internal act that eventually touches action.

### What this is not

This pack does not invite the reader to become a Thelemite, to use a numerical greeting, to join an order, or to perform a rite. The Llewellyn note that people on that path greet with 93 is a reason to **keep 93 off the page** (see `gematria_methods.md`), not a reason to adopt the greeting. “Holy,” in Crowley’s comment, names the austerity of staying on one course (“that austere and holy task”). That is his sentence. It is not the definition of the holy technobook. Joshua’s “holy” is cadence toward the fantastic and the exceptional so that people feel the truth and then know it. The two uses may rhyme. They are not the same doctrine, and this book does not preach his.

## 4. Parallels that sovereignty prose can actually use

These are parallels of structure. They are not identities. The Codex does not become a commentary on *Liber AL*, and *Liber AL* does not become a source of EXR law.

| Thelemic or planetary idea, as read above | Public EXR gesture it can sit under | How the sentence uses it |
|-------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------|--------------------------|
| A star has one orbit. Will must not be two. | The operator is the only actor. One confirm covers one named act (`ARCHITECTURE.md`). | The will-sentence is singular. A second aim in the same sentence is the “will that is two,” and it is cut. |
| Do that, and do nothing else. Swerving hurts. | Drift is a measured distance from the contract. At the bound, halt (`docs/DRIFT.md`). | A passage may feel the swerve before it names the halt. Moon, then Saturn. Not a sermon on punishment. |
| Change in conformity with will, illustrated by writing with ordinary tools. | Intent, then pure tasks, then a typed confirm, then a seal. | Mercury is the pen. The Sun is the stated intent. Saturn is the seal. The tools stay mundane: metal, file, phrase. |
| Constitutional sovereignty: limited, inside rules. | The confirm is a gate. Unproven claims stay goals (`SECURITY.md`, `ARCHITECTURE.md`). | Authority in the prose is bounded. An unbounded boast is not solar. It is noise. |
| Planetary hour: the first hour colors the day, and the day is not made of sixty-minute clock hours. | The contract is stated before labor. Seasons of the work are unequal. | Do not pretend every paragraph costs the same. A Mars sentence is short. A Saturn sentence is short for a different reason (weight). A Jupiter sentence is allowed to widen. |
| Lust of result, as the comment quotes it: pure will delivered from that lust. | The essay’s refusal to call a thing done before it passes: a headless layer that shuts down if it drifts ([essay](https://crowcawl.substack.com/p/your-computer-is-optional-now)). | The knowing sentence reports what passed. It does not beg the reader to be amazed. |
| Love under will, as a phrase in this chapter’s commentary tradition. | Not a romance plot and not a slogan. If it touches EXR at all, it is this: relation follows the true course; sentiment does not steer the desk. | Do not print the phrase. If a bond appears (Venus), it is subordinated to the stated course (the orbit), in that order, still unnamed. |

### A movement that is allowed

1. Moon or Priestess: the reader feels a course that is already theirs, without a definition.
2. Chariot: the course carries them. Two banks, one motion.
3. Sun: one plain sentence of what the course is (authorship of one’s own mind, local metal, a confirm that is theirs).
4. Adjustment: the limit of that course — one act, one phrase, no second will.
5. Saturn / Universe: stop. The seal is the stop, not an extra paragraph of uplift.

### Movements that are refused

- A sentence that tells the reader to do what they wilt, in those words or in a wink at 93.
- A sentence that offers grades, orders, passwords, or a holy guardian as a product feature.
- A sentence that uses “holy” as church. The fantastic is allowed. The dogmatic is not. The public book already says it is not a religion.
- A ritual instruction: hours to cast, names to vibrate, seals to draw. Agrippa and Crowley published those for their own readers. This lattice uses only the public, structural ideas above.
- Any definition of unpublished densify cadence-jargon. Out of scope. The brief’s examples are Nexus and He-Confluence. Do not hang them on a planet to make them look classical.

## 5. Scholarly posture

Name the sources. Do not recruit. Do not mock the sources either: Agrippa’s hours and Crowley’s orbit are precise instruments, and they remain theirs. EXR sovereignty prose borrows the shape — one course, a bounded authority, a proper time, a change made with ordinary tools — and it speaks that shape in the Codex’s own cadence. The reader should feel the course before they are handed a name. Most of the time they are not handed the name at all.
